Abstract

There is substantial geographic variation in life expectancy (LE), especially between small areas. The COVID-19 pandemic caused a mortality crisis, but it is unclear how small-area variations in LE changed during the pandemic. The objective of this study was to produce gender- and period-specific LE estimates by census tract in Philadelphia, PA, over two three-year periods (2017–2019 and 2020–2022), and to compare disparities in LE before and during the pandemic. We estimated mortality rates using a Bayesian Poisson model adaptation of the TOPALS (tool for projecting age patterns using linear splines) that included a conditional autoregressive prior for spatial random effects. We then computed LE and absolute and relative disparities in LE, and compared them between the two periods. Overall, the median [P10–P90] change in LE during the pandemic was -2.32 [-7.94–1.23] and -1.44 [-4.65–1.26] years for men and women, respectively. This decline was geographically patterned in the same areas that had lower LE at baseline. We found widening absolute and relative disparities, mostly due to a steeper decline in LE in the lower income deciles during the pandemic. Our study leveraged Bayesian models to produce precise local-level estimates in small areas, highlighting again the disparate impacts that pandemic had on population health.
